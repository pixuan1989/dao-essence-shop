## What Is the Command Position?

The command position (also called the "power position") is the single most important concept in feng shui desk placement. It comes from ancient Chinese military strategy: a general always sits where they can see the entire battlefield, with their back protected by mountains.

Three conditions must be met:

1. **Solid wall behind you** — symbolizes support, "backing," and stable career foundation
2. **Facing the door** (but not directly in line with it) — you see opportunities coming, but aren't directly hit by rushing energy
3. **Clear view of the room** — you aren't surprised from behind; you have psychological control over your space

> **Modern translation**: In workspace psychology, this is called "prospect-refuge theory." Humans perform best when they can see the entrance but aren't directly exposed to it. A 2023 study in the *Journal of Environmental Psychology* found that employees with their backs to the door reported 23% higher cortisol levels (chronic stress) than those with a wall behind them.

If you can't achieve perfect command position (most offices don't allow it), prioritize in this order:
- ✅ Solid wall behind you (non-negotiable)
- ✅ Ability to see the door (use a small mirror if needed)
- ⚠️ Not directly in line with the door (diagonal is fine)

## 4 Desk Placement Rules That Matter

### 1. Never sit with your back to the door

This is the #1 mistake. When your back faces the door, your nervous system stays in low-grade "threat mode" — you can't fully relax or focus. In feng shui, this is called "backing no support" (无靠).

**The fix**: If you can't move your desk, add a high-backed chair, a cushion, or even a small "wall" (bookshelf behind your chair). In a pinch, wear a suit jacket with a high collar — it creates a psychological "shield."

### 2. Avoid the "coffin position" (directly facing a wall)

Sitting facing a blank wall with no space to look into the room creates "stuck" energy. Your career feels blocked, promotions stall, and motivation drops.

**The fix**: If you must face a wall, hang a landscape painting or mirror on it. The mirror expands the space visually; the landscape gives your eyes a "distance" to look into. Best choice: a painting of water flowing *toward* you (symbolizes wealth flowing in).

### 3. Desk shape matters: curves > sharp corners

L-shaped desks are ideal (symbolizes "embracing" opportunities). Rectangular desks are fine. Avoid desks with sharp corners pointing at you (creates "poison arrow" or sha qi).

**The fix**: If your desk has sharp corners, place a small round plant or a ceramic mug at the corner to "soften" it.

### 4. Keep the left side ("dragon side") higher than the right

In traditional feng shui, the left side represents the "dragon" (career power, male energy, active growth). The right side is the "tiger" (relationships, support, passive energy). For career advancement, keep your left side slightly "heavier" — a taller lamp, a stack of books, or a plant.

**Modern interpretation**: This is similar to the "left-side dominance" principle in visual design. A slightly asymmetric layout feels more dynamic and "active" than a perfectly symmetric one.

## FAQ: Desk Feng Shui Questions Answered

**Q: Where should I face my desk for career luck?**
A: Face your personal lucky direction based on your Kua number (East or West group). If you don't know your Kua number, sit in the command position: facing the door at an angle, with a solid wall behind you.

**Q: Why is desk facing the door bad in feng shui?**
A: A desk directly facing the door creates "rushing energy" (chong sha) — you're exposed and vulnerable. The fix: place your desk diagonally from the door, not in direct line with it.

**Q: What is the command position for desk placement?**
A: Command position = sitting with a solid wall behind you (support), facing the door but not directly in line with it (awareness without exposure), and having a clear view of the room (control).

**Q: Can feng shui desk placement really affect my career?**
A: While scientific evidence is limited, many professionals report improved focus and confidence after adjusting their desk position. The psychological effect of feeling "supported" by a solid wall is well-documented in workspace research.

**Q: What if I can't move my desk at work?**
A: Use workarounds: add a high-backed chair or cushion for 'wall support,' place a small mirror to see the door, or add a plant to the left side for growth energy. Small adjustments still help.
